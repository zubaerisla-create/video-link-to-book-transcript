Most of us have a "watch later" list that only grows, and a handful of videos we've watched more than once because they were genuinely good. A personal digital library is the place for the second group: talks, lectures and interviews kept as readable text, so you can return to them as easily as a book on a shelf. This guide covers what's worth keeping, how to structure a library so it stays useful, and how to make it part of your reading habit rather than another backlog.

## Why turn videos into books at all?

Text has a few advantages over video when you want to keep and revisit something:

- **Rereading is fast.** You can reread a key passage in seconds instead of scrubbing for it.
- **Reading is flexible.** Text works on a commute, in a quiet room, on a small screen, without headphones.
- **Text is searchable.** You can find the passage where someone made a specific point.
- **It's durable.** A plain-text or PDF copy stays readable even if the video is removed, edited or made private.

Video still wins for anything visual — demonstrations, diagrams, code being written. A library doesn't replace the video; it gives you a faster way back into it.

## What deserves a place

A library is only useful if it's selective. A simple test: *would I reread or quote this?* Good candidates:

- Lectures from a course you want to understand properly.
- Long-form interviews with people whose thinking you want to study.
- Talks you keep recommending to others.
- Explanations of something you'll need again — a technical concept, a process, a history.
- Videos in a language you're learning, at a level where reading helps (see [How to Learn English Using YouTube Transcripts](/guides/how-to-learn-english-using-youtube-transcripts)).

Skip news that will date quickly, entertainment you won't revisit, and anything mostly visual.

## Step 1: Capture

1. Extract the transcript on the [Transcriptly home page](/), or with the [Chrome extension](/guides/how-to-use-chrome-extension).
2. Choose **long** paragraphs if you want it to read like a book, **medium** for a mix of reading and skimming.
3. Click **Save to My Library**. You'll need a free account; if you're not signed in, Transcriptly holds the transcript in your browser while you sign up or log in.

You can also add things that didn't come from a YouTube link: on the home page, **Upload / Paste Subtitles** accepts `.srt`, `.vtt` or `.txt` files, or pasted text, and turns them into a readable book.

## Step 2: Give each book a useful title

YouTube titles are written to attract clicks, not to describe content. Rename books in your dashboard so the title tells you what's inside:

| YouTube title | Better library title |
| --- | --- |
| "You've Been Thinking About Memory ALL WRONG" | Memory and spaced repetition — Dr X, 2025 |
| "Lecture 7" | ECON 101 L07 — Price elasticity |
| "We Need To Talk About This…" | Interview: Y on remote team management |

A consistent prefix (a course code, a series name, "Interview:") makes the dashboard's search far more useful — one word brings up the whole group.

## Step 3: Read the way books are read

The Transcriptly reader is built for long reading sessions:

- **Page-by-page or continuous scroll.** Pages give a sense of progress; scrolling is better for skimming.
- **Light, sepia and dark themes**, with adjustable text size for different screens and lighting.
- **Reading progress** is remembered, so you can pick up where you left off across devices.
- **Word lookup.** Tap an English word to see its meaning, pronunciation and a Bangla translation, and save it to your [vocabulary list](/guides/how-to-build-a-vocabulary-dictionary-from-video-transcripts).

Treat a library book like a real one: read with intent. If a passage matters, note its approximate position and check the moment in the video if you need the visual context.

## Step 4: Keep an archive copy outside the service

Any online library can change. For the books you care most about, export a copy:

- **PDF** for a nicely laid-out, printable book with a title page and page numbers (it renders Bangla correctly).
- **TXT** for a plain-text copy that any device can open forever.
- **JSON** if you want timestamps and metadata preserved.

Store exports in a folder you already back up. [SRT vs TXT vs JSON](/guides/srt-vs-txt-vs-json-transcript-formats) helps you choose.

## Step 5: Curate regularly

A library that only grows becomes a pile. Once a month or so:

- **Delete** books you've finished and won't revisit. Deleting a book in Transcriptly removes it from your account permanently, so export first if you might want it.
- **Rename** anything whose title no longer makes sense.
- **Pick one book to reread.** Rereading the best material is the main reason to have a library.

## Organising by purpose

As your library grows, it helps to think in shelves, expressed through title prefixes:

- **Courses** — lecture series in order: `COURSE Lxx — Topic`.
- **People** — interviews and talks by the same speaker: `Speaker — Topic (Year)`.
- **Reference** — explanations you'll look up: `How-to: …`, `Explainer: …`.
- **Language practice** — videos in your target language: `[BN] …`, `[EN] …`.

For larger collections or research use, the system in [How to Extract and Organize YouTube Video Transcripts](/guides/how-to-extract-and-organize-youtube-transcripts) adds a spreadsheet index and file-naming scheme.

## Common mistakes

- **Saving everything.** A library of 500 unread transcripts is just a longer "watch later" list.
- **Keeping click-bait titles.** You won't recognise them in six months.
- **Relying on a single copy.** Export the ones that matter.
- **Forgetting where it came from.** The source URL is stored with each saved book and included in exports — keep it when you rename or reformat.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I edit the text of a saved book?

You can rename books. To correct the text itself, export it, edit the file, and if you want it back in your library, upload the corrected `.txt` through **Upload / Paste Subtitles** on the home page.

### Is my library private?

Yes. Saved books are tied to your account and are only visible when you're signed in. See the [Privacy Policy](/privacy) for details.

### Can I read offline?

The reader needs a connection. For offline reading, export a PDF or TXT to your device.

### Is it legal to keep transcripts of other people's videos?

Keeping a private copy for your own study is generally treated very differently from republishing it. Don't redistribute full transcripts without permission; see our [copyright page](/copyright).
